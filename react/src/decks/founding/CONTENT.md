## XI · Structure ◆

Revised from the reference deck at the client's instruction — reverses where IP sits (Delaware parent instead of Dubai parent with India delivery), so the counter-argument ships on the slide itself.

**Eyebrow:** DEFENSIBLE BY DESIGN
**Title:** Delaware first. *Dubai shortly after.*
**Lede:** A US parent matches the near-term counterparty and the standard instruments. The Gulf entity follows for the corridor, the client-facing lane and proximity to the capital.

Entity tree:
- **Delaware C corp** (NOW) — Parent · IP-owning. Employer of record for founders, holder of the models and platform.
- **DIFC entity** (+6–9 MONTHS) — Client-facing · Gulf & CIS. English common law, the deepest private-markets ecosystem in the Gulf.
- **India delivery** (AS DEMAND LANDS) — Build at scale. Wholly-owned delivery centre under an intercompany services agreement.

Rationale:
1. The counterparty is American — founding partners and the first institutional round expect a Delaware C corp, a SAFE or a priced round, and no explanation.
2. The QSBS clock starts at incorporation — the holding period only ever runs from the day the C corp exists. Every month of delay is unrecoverable. *(Eligibility and treatment pending counsel.)*
3. Data rights engineered up front — a formal data-and-IP agreement is the founding document — abstracted process templates and eval sets, not raw client files.
4. A clean, unregulated lane — we never make investment decisions, solicit LPs, or give regulated advice — that stays with the GP, by design.

Counter-argument (on the slide, deliberately): "This moves IP ownership from the Gulf to Delaware — the opposite of the prior structure. If the Gulf and CIS corridor is the real revenue engine rather than the delivery route, a Dubai parent is the better answer and this slide should be rebuilt."

Pending note: "Structure and tax treatment pending counsel. Not tax advice."
